## Research Project Spring 2024

**Geometric MG for Elliptic and $H(\text{div})$ Equations**  

*Proposed by Panayot S. Vassilevski on April 17, 2024*

### Problem Formulation

We use NGSolve to generate for a given 2D polygonal domain $\Omega$ a sequence of triangular finite element meshes by uniform refinement:  
$$
\mathcal{T}_0 \subset \mathcal{T}_1 \subset \cdots \subset \mathcal{T}_{\ell}
$$

We use two types of f.e. spaces:  

- $H^1$-conforming piecewise linear
- $H(\text{div})$-conforming lowest order Raviart-Thomas.

The refinement procedure should give the respective interpolation matrices ($P_k$ or $\mathbf{P}_k$, see below) that map a coefficient vector from $k$ th level space into the $k+1$-th finer level space.  That is, we have

- $P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_{\ell - 1}$ in the piecewise linear case.
- $\mathbf{P}_0, \mathbf{P}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{P}_{\ell - 1}$ for the $H(\text{div})$-space. We may also need the piecewise constant interpolants $Q_0,Q_1,\ldots,Q_{\ell - 1}$ for the scalar spaces of discontinuous piecewise constants $W_0,W_1,\ldots,W_{\ell - 1}$.

We consider two bilinear forms:

- elliptic one:
    $$
    a(u,v) = (u,v) + \tau(\nabla u, \nabla v), \qquad u,v \in H^1(\Omega).
    $$

    Above, $\tau$ is a given positive parameter.
- the $H(\text{div})$ one:
    $$
    a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}) = (\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}) + \tau(\text{div }  \mathbf{u},\text{div } \mathbf{v}),\qquad \mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in H(\text{div};\Omega).
    $$

    Again, here $\tau$ is a given positive parameter.

We compute the respective stiffness matrices $A_k$ and mass matrices $G_k$ for $k=0,1,\ldots,\ell$ in the elliptic case, and $\mathbf{A}_k$ and $\mathbf{G}_k$ in the $H(\text{div})$ case. They should satisfy the Galerkin (variational) relations:
$$
A_k = P_k^T A_{k+1} P_k ,\qquad \mathbf{A}_k = \mathbf{P}_k^T \mathbf{A}_{k+1} \mathbf{P}_k.
$$

The same holds for the mass matrices:
$$
G_k = P_k^T G_{k+1} P_k, \qquad \mathbf{G}_k = \mathbf{P}_k^T \mathbf{G}_{k+1} \mathbf{P}_k.
$$

### A Multigrid Algorithm

We consider first the elliptic case. We are given the sequence of matrices $\left\{A_k\right\}$ and respective interpolation matrices $\left\{P_k\right\}$, and we choose for each $A_k$ a convergent smoother $M_k$ (e.g., forward Gauss-Seidel, or the diagonal $\ell_1$-smoother).  

A standard MG algorithm is defined by recursion in terms of the inverse action of a mapping $B_k$ at every level $k$. That is, given a vector $\mathbf{b}_k$, the actions $B_k^{-1}\mathbf{b}_k$ are computed as follows:

- If $k=0$ (the coarsest level), we let $B_0^{-1} = A_0^{-1}$, that is, $\mathbf{x}_0 = B_0^{-1}\mathbf{b}_0$ is computed by solving exactly $A_0\mathbf{x}_0 = \mathbf{b}_0$.
- Once the actions of $B_{k-1}^{-1}$ for $k\geq 1$ are available, we compute
    $$
    \mathbf{x} = B_k^{-1} \mathbf{b},
    $$
    in the following steps:
    - pre-smooth:
        $$
        \mathbf{x} = M_k^{-1}\mathbf{b}.
        $$
    - restrict residual $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{b}-A_k\mathbf{x}$, i.e., compute
        $$
        \mathbf{b}_c = P_{k-1}^T \mathbf{r},
        $$
        and use $B_{k-1}^{-1}$ to compute
        $$
        \mathbf{x}_c = B_{k-1}^{-1}\mathbf{b}_c.
        $$
    - Interpolate the coarse-level correction and update
        $$
        \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x} + P_{k-1}\mathbf{x}_c.
        $$
    - post-smooth:
        $$
        \mathbf{z} = M^{-T}(\mathbf{b}-A_k \mathbf{x}).
        $$
    - and finally, update current $\mathbf{x}$, i.e.,
        $$
        \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{z},
        $$
        which is the desired output $B_k^{-1}\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{x}$.

The actions of $B^{-1}=B_{MG}^{-1}=B_{\ell}^{-1}$ are used in a stationary iterative method for solving $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ $(A=A_{\ell})$ as follows:  

Starting with an initial iterate $\mathbf{x}$ (zero or random), at every new iteration step we compute
$$
\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{b} - A\mathbf{x},\\\\
\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x} + B^{-1}\mathbf{r}.
$$

We stop the iterations after the current residual norm $\delta = \left\| \mathbf{r}\right\|_{B^{-1}}$ and the initial one $\delta_0 = \left\|\mathbf{r}_0 \right\|_{B^{-1}}$ satisfy a stopping criterion
$$
\delta \leq \epsilon \delta_0, \qquad \left(\epsilon = 10^{-6}\right),
$$
or we have reached a maximal number of iterations allowed.  

Since the mapping $B$ is s.p.d., it can be used as a preconditioner in the conjugate gradient method. In fact, the following recursive representation holds
$$
B^{-1} = \overline{M}^{-1} + \left(I - M^{-T} A\right) P B_c^{-1} P^T \left(I - AM^{-1}\right),
$$
which relates a coarse $B_c$ and the next fine level $B$.  

Here, $\overline{M} = M\left(M+M^T - A\right)^{-1}M^T$ is the so-called symmetrized smoother, which in fact is symmetric Gauss-Seidel if $M$ is the forward Gauss-Seidel corresponding to $A$. (Note that $M + M^T - A$ is the diagonal of $A$ in that case).  

There is a fact also, that if $M$ is a convergent smoother for $A$ in the $A$-norm, then a necessary and sufficient condition for that to hold is that $M + M^T - A$ to be s.p.d. (which is the case for the Gauss-Seidel one).
